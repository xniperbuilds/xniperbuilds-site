Privacy Policy

Riplox Desktop · Last updated: 25 August 2026

Riplox Desktop is a free, open-source video downloader for Windows, published by XniperBuilds under the GPL v3. It runs on your own PC, and this policy covers the program, its browser extension, and the Send feature that lets your phone hand a link to your PC.

The short version: No account, no ads, and no analytics of any kind. We run no servers that receive your data. Your settings, history and downloaded files stay on your PC. When you send a link from your phone, it travels inside an envelope that is encrypted before it leaves the phone — the relay that carries it cannot read what is inside.

Data we collect

None. There is no sign-up and no login to Riplox. We do not collect your name, email, phone number, location, IP address, download history or usage analytics, and we operate no servers that receive personal data. The program contains no analytics or telemetry code, no advertising SDK, and no third-party trackers — it shows no ads at all. It is open source, so this is checkable rather than something you have to take our word for.

Data stored on your PC

To work at all, Riplox keeps a few things on your own computer only:

  • Settings — your download folder, chosen quality, and the rest of your preferences.
  • Download history and queue — the title, link and file location of what you have downloaded, so you can find or repeat it. You can delete any entry or clear the list.
  • The pairing with your phone — a key shared between your phone and this PC, if you use Send.
  • Site sign-in, only if you choose it — if you sign in to a site so Riplox can fetch something that needs an account, those cookies are stored encrypted with Windows DPAPI, one file per site, so a copied file is useless on another machine. Signing out of one site removes only that file.
  • Downloaded files — saved wherever you point Riplox. These are your files and Riplox never sends them anywhere.

Ordinarily this lives in your Windows user folder. If you run the portable build, all of it stays in a Data folder beside the program instead, and nothing is written elsewhere on the PC.

Network use

Riplox connects to the internet for four things, and nothing else:

  • The site you asked for — to fetch the video you pasted or sent.
  • Keeping its download engine current — Riplox uses the open-source yt-dlp and updates its own copy so links keep working when sites change.
  • Checking whether a newer Riplox exists — it asks GitHub's public releases API for the latest version number. Nothing about you is sent with that request.
  • The Send relay, described next, and only if you turn Send on.

Send, and what the relay can see

Send lets you share a link on your phone and have the download start on your PC. It also carries plain text the same way — a key, a password, a Wi-Fi code — which arrives on the PC hidden until you copy it, and is then gone. Everything below applies equally to both.

It works two ways:

  • On the same Wi-Fi, the link goes straight from your phone to your PC across your own network. It never reaches us, and it works with no internet connection at all.
  • Anywhere else, it passes through our relay at relay.xniperbuilds.com. The link is sealed with AES-GCM using the key your phone and PC agreed at pairing, before it leaves the phone. The relay holds and forwards that sealed envelope; it does not have the key and cannot read the link, the quality you chose, or anything else inside.

The relay keeps a message only until your PC collects it. We do not log the contents of messages, because we could not read them if we tried.

The browser extension

The Riplox extension adds a button that sends the page you are looking at to Riplox on the same PC.

  • It reads the address of the current tab, and only when you press the button or use the Riplox right-click item. It does not watch your browsing, and it does not read page content.
  • That address is passed to Riplox on your own computer through Chrome's native-messaging channel. It does not go to us or to any website.
  • It stores only which PC it should talk to. It has no account, sends no analytics, and shows no ads.

Third parties

Riplox Desktop bundles no advertising or analytics services. It is built on the open-source projects yt-dlp and FFmpeg, which run on your machine. When you download something, you are of course contacting the site it is hosted on, and that site's own privacy policy applies to that connection. We do not sell data to anyone, because we do not have any.

Children

Riplox Desktop is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal data from anyone.
