Send a link from your phone — the PC can be switched off

The part of Riplox that browser tools and other downloaders do not do.

You find something on your phone. The video belongs on the computer — the big screen, the drive with room on it, the folder everything else lives in. So you email yourself the link, or message it to yourself, and later you remember to go and fetch it. Usually you don't.

Riplox has a postbox instead. Share the link from your phone the way you share anything else and it goes into a queue. If the PC is on, the download starts within seconds. If the PC is off, asleep, or in another city, the link waits — and the moment Riplox is running again, everything that piled up downloads at once. Share eleven videos overnight and eleven files are waiting in the morning.

This is worth being precise about, because most tools that claim to connect a phone and a PC need both ends awake, on the same Wi-Fi, and able to find each other. Riplox needs none of that. There is no discovery step, no network to join, and nothing to leave open. The link is encrypted on your phone and only your PC can read it — the relay in between carries a sealed envelope and cannot open it.

What you need

A 64-bit Windows 10 or Windows 11 PC. That is the whole list. Riplox installs for your user account, so it never asks for administrator rights, and there is no server, no subscription and no account of any kind. Older Windows is not supported, and setup says so plainly rather than installing something that cannot start.

What it does not do

It does not upload anything about you, show ads, or bundle a second program with the installer. It does not download by itself — watching a channel notifies you, and you decide. It will not get past a paywall or reach a private video your own account cannot see, and it is not a torrent client or a general file downloader. Those are deliberate gaps, not missing features.

Instagram and TikTok on the PC

Riplox Desktop takes Instagram and TikTok links alongside everything else, so there is no need for a separate Instagram video downloader for PC or a second app for TikTok. The two Android apps exist for the phone, where a share sheet beats copying a link. On Windows, one app covers all of it.

Questions people ask first

Is it really free, or is there a paid version later?

Free, and there is no other version. No download caps, no daily quota, no trial that expires, no account. The source is published under the GPL-3.0 licence, which means it stays that way — anyone can read exactly what it does, and nobody can take it closed.

Does it work on Windows 10, or only Windows 11?

Both, on 64-bit, and it behaves the same on each. Setup checks the version before writing anything, so an older PC is told plainly instead of ending up with an app that will not start.

Windows warned me about the file. Should I be worried?

That warning appears because the installer is not signed with a code-signing certificate yet — not because anything was found in it. SmartScreen shows it for any new program without an established reputation. Every release page publishes the SHA-256 checksum, which is how you confirm the download is the file we built.

Where do the files go, and in what format?

Wherever you point it — the folder is yours to choose and it is remembered. Video is saved as H.264 MP4 by default so it plays in Windows Media Player, on phones and in WhatsApp without hunting for codecs. Audio comes out as MP3. You pick the quality each time, from 4K down to 360p.

A site changed and a download stopped working. Now what?

Sites change how they serve video, and when that happens it is the download engine that needs updating, not the whole app. One click updates it in place — no waiting for a new release, no reinstall.

Does it keep downloading if I close the window?

Yes. Closing the window sends Riplox to the tray and everything carries on, with progress on the taskbar button and a notification as each file lands. Quitting from the tray is what actually stops it.

If I uninstall it, do I lose my queue and settings?

Only if you ask to. Uninstalling asks whether to remove your settings, sign-ins, history and unfinished queue as well, and the default answer is no — so reinstalling or upgrading leaves everything where you left it. Downloaded files are never touched either way.
